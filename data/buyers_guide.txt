UNDERSTANDING PROPERTY TYPES

In the Indian real estate market, properties are broadly classified into several categories. Apartments and flats are the most common type of residential property in urban areas. They are part of larger multi-storey buildings managed by a housing society. Independent houses and bungalows offer more privacy and personal space. They come with their own land and are typically found in suburban areas. Villas are premium detached homes, usually found in gated communities, offering luxury amenities like private gardens, swimming pools, and high-end finishes. Plots and land refer to vacant land parcels that buyers purchase to construct their own homes. Commercial properties include office spaces, retail shops, and co-working spaces intended for business use rather than residential living.


WHAT IS A HOMEOWNERS ASSOCIATION (HOA)?

A Homeowners Association, commonly referred to as an HOA or housing society in India, is an organization that manages and maintains shared spaces and amenities within a residential community or apartment complex. When you purchase a property in a gated community or apartment building, you automatically become a member of the HOA. The HOA collects monthly maintenance fees from all homeowners. These fees cover common expenses such as security personnel, cleaning of common areas, lift maintenance, water supply management, garden upkeep, swimming pool maintenance, and building insurance. Monthly HOA fees in India typically range from Rs 2,000 to Rs 15,000 depending on the amenities and location. Some premium communities can charge Rs 25,000 or more per month. It is important to understand the HOA rules before purchasing, as they may include restrictions on renovations, pet ownership, rental policies, and noise levels.


UNDERSTANDING MORTGAGE AND HOME LOANS IN INDIA

A home loan or mortgage is a secured loan provided by a bank or financial institution to help you purchase a property. The property itself serves as collateral for the loan. In India, home loans are available from public sector banks like SBI and PNB, private banks like HDFC and ICICI, and housing finance companies like LIC Housing Finance and Bajaj Housing Finance.

There are primarily two types of interest rates. Fixed rate loans maintain the same interest rate throughout the loan tenure, giving you predictable monthly EMIs. Floating rate loans have interest rates that fluctuate based on the Reserve Bank of India's repo rate changes. Most Indian banks currently offer home loan interest rates between 8.5 percent and 10.5 percent per annum. The maximum loan tenure is typically 30 years, and banks usually finance 75 to 90 percent of the property value, meaning you need to arrange the remaining 10 to 25 percent as a down payment from your own funds.

Key documents required for a home loan application include identity proof such as Aadhaar and PAN card, address proof, income proof including salary slips and IT returns for the last 2 to 3 years, bank statements for the last 6 months, property documents, and passport-sized photographs. Having a good CIBIL credit score of 750 or above significantly improves your chances of loan approval and getting a lower interest rate.


HOME INSPECTION CHECKLIST

Before finalizing any property purchase, it is crucial to conduct a thorough home inspection. For new construction properties, check the RERA registration number and verify it on the state RERA website. Inspect the quality of construction including walls, flooring, electrical fittings, plumbing fixtures, and paint finishing. Test all taps, flush systems, and drainage to ensure proper water flow. Check that all electrical switches, sockets, and light points are functional. Verify the actual carpet area matches what was promised in the agreement. For resale properties, additionally check for water seepage, cracks in walls, termite damage, and the overall structural condition.

Always verify that the property has a clear title with no legal disputes. Check the encumbrance certificate to ensure there are no pending loans or liabilities on the property. Verify the approved building plan with the local municipal authority. Ensure the property has received an occupancy certificate from the local development authority.


UNDERSTANDING STAMP DUTY AND REGISTRATION

In India, every property transaction requires payment of stamp duty and registration charges to the state government. Stamp duty is a tax levied on the transfer of property ownership and varies from state to state. In West Bengal, the current stamp duty rate is 6 to 7 percent of the property value for properties in urban areas and 5 to 6 percent in rural areas. Registration charges are an additional 1 to 2 percent. In Maharashtra, stamp duty is 5 percent in Mumbai and 6 percent in the rest of the state. Karnataka charges 5 percent stamp duty plus 1 percent registration. Female buyers often get a concession of 1 to 2 percent on stamp duty in several states.

The total cost of stamp duty and registration can add 7 to 10 percent to your property purchase price. This is a significant amount and should be factored into your budget from the beginning. Stamp duty must be paid before or at the time of execution of the sale deed. You can pay stamp duty online through the state government portal or through franking at authorized centres.


CLOSING COSTS AND HIDDEN CHARGES

Beyond the property price and stamp duty, buyers should budget for several additional costs. Brokerage fees are typically 1 to 2 percent of the property value if you are using a real estate agent. Home loan processing fees charged by banks range from 0.25 to 1 percent of the loan amount. Legal fees for property verification and documentation can cost between Rs 5,000 to Rs 25,000. GST at 5 percent is applicable on under-construction properties but not on ready-to-move-in properties. If buying in a new project, you may need to pay a Preferential Location Charge (PLC) for corner units, higher floors, or units facing a park. Advance maintenance charges for 12 to 24 months are often collected by builders at the time of possession. Moving costs, interior design, and furnishing are additional expenses that can add 5 to 15 percent to your total outlay.


TIPS FOR FIRST-TIME HOME BUYERS

Start by getting pre-approved for a home loan so you know your budget before you start looking at properties. Research the neighbourhood thoroughly including connectivity to your workplace, proximity to schools and hospitals, availability of public transport, and future infrastructure developments planned in the area. Compare at least 3 to 5 properties before making a decision. Never skip the legal verification process even if the property looks perfect. Consider the resale value and rental potential of the property for long-term investment purposes. Always negotiate the price as listed prices often have a margin of 5 to 10 percent. Read all documents carefully before signing, especially the sale agreement and builder-buyer agreement. Keep copies of all documents and payment receipts safely.
